# Tiny Swords tilemap

Every `Terrain/Tileset/Tilemap_colorN.png` of the Tiny Swords pack is a 576×384 sheet of
64×64 cells, 9 columns by 6 rows. The five colour sheets share one layout, so a tile
index means the same piece in each of them. The index is `row * 9 + col`, from 0 at the
top left to 53 at the bottom right.
In this page a cell is a 64×64 position on the sheet, and a tile is the piece drawn in
a cell; ten cells hold no tile. A position on the game map is a map tile.

The client names the tiles in `static/tileset.js`. `TILESET_TILE` maps each non-empty
tile to its index, and `G.locateTilesetTile(index)` turns an index into the top-left
corner of its cell in the sheet. `static/map_image.js` picks its tiles through these
names, so no pixel offset is written by hand.

## Regenerate

```bash
uv run --script scripts/tileset_reference.py
```

The script reads `TILESET_TILE` from `static/tileset.js` and rewrites the table below.
It also draws `docs/tileset_reference.png`: `Tilemap_color1.png` scaled ×2, with each
index printed in its cell and the empty cells marked "empty". Git ignores that image
because it is derived from the pack, whose licence forbids redistributing the assets
(issue #134), so generate it locally when you need to see the tiles.

## Layout

Flat ground fills columns 0 to 3 and elevated ground fills columns 5 to 8. Column 4 is
an empty divider, and the two ramps leave cells 37, 38, 46 and 47 empty. Both grounds
use the same 16 shapes, named after the neighbours a tile has:

- a 3×3 block of corners, edges and centre (rows 0 to 2, three columns);
- a vertical strip one tile wide (fourth column, rows 0 to 2);
- a horizontal run one tile high (row 3, three columns);
- an island with no neighbour of its kind (row 3, fourth column).

Below the elevated ground, row 4 holds the cliff faces that stand on walkable ground
and row 5 the same faces standing in water. Ramps sit in columns 0 and 3 of rows 4
and 5, as a top tile over a bottom tile.

The client uses the sheets by terrain, not by elevation (`static/sprites.js`):
`Tilemap_color2` for meadow and lake grass, `Tilemap_color3` for forest grass,
`Tilemap_color1` for highland grass, `Tilemap_color4` for dirt and `Tilemap_color5`
for sand. Today it draws only `FLAT_CENTER` (index 10) from each.

## How the unsure tiles were settled

The research for issue #128 left two pairs of tiles unsure. Both were settled on
2026-11-28 by looking at `Tilemap_color1.png` zoomed ×2 and by comparing with the
guide's semantic ids.

**Indices 23 to 26 against 32 to 35.** The research called the first group a grass lip
and the second a grass-on-rock lip. On the image, 23 to 25 are the bottom row of the
3×3 elevated block: their top edge continues the tiles above, and rock shows under
the grass at their foot. Tiles 32 to 34 have a rounded top edge instead, because they
form a run one tile high, and 35 is rounded on every side. The guide gives them the
same semantic ids as the flat shapes in the same cells (EG7 to EG9 and EG15 against
EG10 to EG12 and EG16). The names therefore follow the shape, as for flat ground:
`ELEVATED_BOTTOM_*` and `ELEVATED_HORIZONTAL_*`, `ELEVATED_STRIP_BOTTOM` and
`ELEVATED_ISLAND`. The guide's rule for a south drop fits this reading. It keeps the
bottom-edge tile, and switches to the horizontal run only when the map tile to the north
is water, which is when the tile has no neighbour to the north.

**Ramps 36 and 45 against 39 and 48.** In column 0, the slope rises from the bottom
left to the top right, and its straight east edge is where it leans on the cliff.
Column 3 is the mirror image. The guide calls column 0 the left ramp, and its rules
place that ramp west of the platform corner, so "left" names the side of the platform
the ramp stands on. The names keep the guide's word: `RAMP_LEFT_*` stands on the west
side and climbs east, and `RAMP_RIGHT_*` stands on the east side and climbs west.

## Elevation facts for issue #129

These facts come from the sources below and are not used by the game yet.

- **Draw order.** From bottom to top: background colour, water foam, flat ground, then
  shadow and elevated ground once per elevation level. The official guide states no
  limit on the number of levels.
- **Levels and colours.** The community guide uses `Tilemap_color1` for level 0, next
  to water, up to `Tilemap_color5` for level 4. This differs from how the client picks
  sheets today (see Layout).
- **A cliff takes two rows.** The elevated map tile keeps its bottom-edge tile, and the
  cliff face goes on the map tile below it. The face is a row 4 tile when that lower map tile
  is land, and a row 5 tile, which has foam at its foot, when it is water. A face has
  a rounded end only on a side whose neighbour on that row is strictly lower.
- **Cliffs face south only.** The art has no north, east or west cliff, so a drop in
  those directions needs water between the levels. The vertical-strip tiles of the
  higher map tile can also cover the seam. Land drops by one level at most.
- **Ramps.** A ramp spans exactly one level, as a top tile over a bottom tile, and its
  bottom must land on ground, never on water. The left ramp touches the platform
  corner at its north-east (`col + 1, row - 1` from the bottom tile), the right ramp
  at its north-west. The platform edge it touches becomes `ELEVATED_CENTER`, and a
  cliff end next to a ramp becomes `CLIFF_FACE_MIDDLE_*`. `CLIFF_FACE_MIDDLE_LAND`
  can be drawn behind the ramp top when the map tile two rows north is at platform level.
  On walkable ground the official guide uses one elevated centre tile at the top of
  the stairs; against a cliff it uses two tiles, the walkable edge and the cliff foot.
- **Shadow.** `Shadow.png` (192×192, a 128×128 blob) sits under the walkable elevated
  area, one tile lower. The community guide draws it only where the map tile to the south
  is lower land and the one after that is land too.
- **Foam.** `Water Foam.png` has 16 frames of 192×192, played at 10 frames a second.
  It is centred on every level-0 tile next to water, never under elevated ground or
  on open water. Each foam sprite starts on a different frame so the waves do not
  move in sync.
